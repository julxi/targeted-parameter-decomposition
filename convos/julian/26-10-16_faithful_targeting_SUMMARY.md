## Overview: where Exp 4e stands (26-10-21, paused)

**What 4e is.** tPD of the MLP output projections (`down_proj`) of Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct layers 11–17 at every statement token of "<statement>\nThe amount of truth in this statement is ", with the KL of the verdict digit ("1" true / "0" false) at the answer as target loss, plus Julian's global orthogonality term ⟨C, Δx⟩ = 0 (C = the summed write of all components, Δx = the remainder of the MLP output), which stops components and Δ from cancelling each other. Tuned on the tiu v2 validation split (986 statements) with 3,000-step runs without PGD, one seed per setting unless noted. *KL recovered* = how much of the verdict the CI-weighted components alone restore (Δ off): 1 = original verdict, 0 = MLPs 11–17 switched off at the statement tokens.

**Main results** [verified unless tagged; details in the sections below and the LOG]:
1. **Tuning**: LR 2e-3 and importance-minimality coefficient 1e-3 are best by Julian's objective: KL recovered 0.91 in two seeds, verdict accuracy 0.97 (original 0.97). No run shows 4a-style cancellation (|Δx| ≤ 1.03 |y|, all components together ≤ 0.66 |y|); small verdict-relevant opposition between components and Δ remains (seed 0's always-on components, L12/L14 in A8).
2. **What the components carry**: their statement-specific activity is necessary (activations frozen to their averages: KL recovered ≈ 0.05–0.07). Without it the model calls false statements "true" (a "true" default); the components supply "this is false". The individually important components are mostly sentence-form detectors (negation, comparison) plus a few false-selective ones; which component does what differs between seeds, the function does not.
3. **At layer 11 that "false" signal is a real part of the model's own MLP output**: removing the components' write subspace from the original output (no decomposition involved) flips 22–28% of false statements to "true", more than removing the whole output; random subspaces of the same size do nothing (A8, both seeds).
4. **The sparsity coefficient changes what the decomposition is** (table below): 3e-4 and 1e-3 cover every family; 3e-3 keeps only components that fire on false statements (removing them makes 75% of false statements read "true", true ones stay true); 1e-2 keeps 8 components, among them L11:22, without which the larger_than verdicts invert. Julian: there is no "right number" of components [C: comment]; choose the decomposition by question.
5. **Reprogramming**: a fixed weight edit along a decomposition's components swaps the verdict on held-out statements (≈ 60 terms → accuracy 0.12, ≈ 250 → 0.05), but an untrained basis aligned with the MLP inputs does it about as well (129 terms → 0.06). Decompositions are slightly better only with ≤ 20 terms and in the affirmative fact families. So swapping does not tell the decompositions apart, and the belief-shaped objective gives only "verdict by sentence form": no belief lever.

## Brainstorm (26-10-16) [assumed unless tagged: agent's analysis]

- Δ is already randomly masked on target data [verified: code], so 4a's freedom comes from the lens: the verdict sees `'.\n'` only through later attention reads. A fix needs a constraint on the internal state at the decomposed position [concluded: architecture].
- Alternatives kept for later: 4b with reconstruction coefficient 0.1 as the cheap baseline; a sensitivity-weighted reconstruction (y − A)ᵀ(λI + G)(y − A) with G the covariance of the verdict gradient; reconstructing within-family true/false differences; post-hoc sparse selection of 4b/4c's components; mean instead of zero as the off value.
- Proposed targetedness metrics: cancellation test, the share of active components whose removal changes the verdict, the components' vs Δ's share of the truth write, the share of all-off KL recovered.

## Exp 4d: the orthogonality penalty (26-10-20)

- **Form** [decided: Julian, transcribed from chat]: one condition for all components together, ⟨A, y − A⟩ = 0, in the CI-weighted state (A = Σ_c CI_c · component write, Δ off; y = original MLP output). Julian prefers this to the per-component form the agent had recommended, so that the components may interact.
- **Metric** [concluded]: plain Euclidean, per position divided by |y|². Checked on the H100 [verified: 300 test statements]: at `'.\n'` the largest channel (2570, the massive-activation channel) holds a median of 1.4–17.4% of |y|² depending on the layer, so no channel dominates. Per-channel std normalisation (agent's earlier idea) [superseded] for that reason, and because with centring it would no longer be about y = A + (y − A).
- **Geometry** [concluded: algebra]: on the sphere, a write along any direction u is limited to y's own coordinate along u (|A| = |y|·|cos(u, y)|), which caps 4a's layer-11 write at 0.03 |y|. The part of A orthogonal to y can still reach |y|/2, as for any genuine part t of y = t + r with t ⟂ r. A partial copy t·y is pushed outward (cost (t(1 − t))²), not shrunk. The penalty (⟨A, y − A⟩/|y|²)² grows like (|A|/|y|)⁴ near A = 0: 4a's write would cost ~3·10⁴, a 0.3 |y| write along a direction y has nothing of only ~0.008. So the coefficient decides whether small and medium writes are blocked.
- **Tuning** [assumed: proposal]: change only the new coefficient, keep everything else at 4a's values. Treat the term as a constraint: take the smallest coefficient at which the cancellation test shows no re-implementation, then the best verdict recovery. Coefficients a decade apart behave differently (r⁴), so 1 and 10 are run. If more values are needed: replace the coefficient by a tolerance with an adaptive Lagrange multiplier (not implemented). One seed per run.
- **Code** [verified: tests incl. a mutation check]: `spd/metrics/orthogonality_loss.py` (loss and eval metric; the eval also logs mean signed ⟨A, y − A⟩/|y|² and |A|/|y| per layer), `ComponentOrthogonalityLossConfig` in `spd/configs.py`, new `pre_weight_acts` argument of `compute_losses` (A and y from the original forward's module inputs, as in the cancellation test; no extra forward), excluded from the Pile losses, WandB short name `CompOrtho`. Tests: `tests/test_orthogonality_loss.py`, `tests/test_metric_short_names.py` (every metric needs a short name; its absence crashed the first trial). Config: `spd/experiments/lm/honesty_targeted_decomposition/config_truth_sf_verdict_ortho.yaml` (= 4a + term at coefficient 1).
- **Trial** (300 steps, `s-37397e7d`) [verified: one run]: stable, 3:21 on the H100. The components write tiny vectors (CI-weighted |A|/|y| 0.014–0.030 at step 300), penalty 10⁻⁶–10⁻³. With rounded CI the active components write 0.07–0.17 |y| at cos(A, y) ≈ 0. 30% of the active CIs lie strictly between 0.01 and 0.99, so rounded and CI-weighted A differ by about 2×.
- **Result at coefficient 1** (`s-5c284ebb`; its checkpoint was deleted from WandB on 26-10-21 at Julian's request, no copy left, analysis outputs kept; all 1,794 test statements, statement end; one seed) [verified]: components only KL 0.147 (24% of the all-off KL 0.193 recovered; CI-weighted 0.187, 3%), accuracy 0.884 (original 0.978, all off 0.855); **Δ only KL 0.027, accuracy 0.960**; 2.3 active components per statement; components write 0.04–0.17 |y| at cos(A, y) ≈ 0; inactive components and Δ each 3–11 |y|, cancelling. For comparison: 4a components KL 0.011 (94%) via re-implementation, 4b 0.023 (88%). The training's stochastic verdict loss equals 4a's (0.0087 vs 0.0082), so tPD's loss at this position does not force the verdict into components. Reading [concluded]: the penalty removed the re-implementation by pushing the components to the trivial corner A ≈ 0. The constraint is not binding at the end (penalty 0.0009), so the coefficient-10 run was stopped after ~4 min: it could only push further towards 0. Outputs: `~/spd_out/26-10-16_faithful_targeting/full/` (scripts, logs, `exp4d_analysis.py` results).
- **Julian's follow-up (26-10-20): condition on all components, ⟨C, y − C⟩ = ⟨C, Δx⟩ = 0** (C = write of all components at mask 1; no threshold, no CI dependence). Agent's answer [concluded unless tagged]: the active/inactive split is learned, so a condition on A alone can be dodged by moving the cancellation into the inactive components, as 4d did. Estimated from the per-layer mean norms, ⟨C, Δ⟩/|y|² is −183 to −11 in 4a, −120 to −9 in 4d, ≈ 0 in 4b, so the C-condition flags both failures with large penalties. Alone it allows A ≈ −I, so the agent proposes both terms together (bounds |A_ci|, |C|, |Δx| ≤ |y| and |I_ci| ≤ 2|y|). Neither term forces the verdict into the components (C = 0, Δ = y satisfies both). The agent proposed 4a + both terms [superseded: Julian chose the global term alone, next section]; its prediction that the C-term would dominate early training was wrong (it started at 0.022).
